# UI copy

UI copy works like highway signs: say exactly what the reader needs, in as few words as possible.

## Surfaces

- Cut fields before wording them. A panel state gets the fewest elements that still work, such
  as one line plus one action button.
- Prefer fewer, larger strings over more, smaller ones.
- Drop an eyebrow that repeats the heading or body. In product panels this overrides the
  light-enterprise-ui item that puts an eyebrow over every section; keep one only where it names
  a topic the body does not.

## Strings

- Every string a page shows (lead, note, hint, empty state, status, caption, error) is one clause
  with no comma and no period, in the reader's terms.
- No jargon, no parentheses, no emojis. Use a plain exclamation point for enthusiasm: "6
  notebooks, ready to go!"
- Headings are standard nouns: "Results for [query]", "Recipients", "Gifts", "New event". Never a
  customized heading such as "5 that fit" or "A gift for your guests".
- Data surfaces (overlay labels, list rows, counts, status tags) take bare annotations: `bridge
  84%`, an `acceptable` tag with a count. Counts and dates are bare: "22 items", "Due Oct 22".
- A label never recaps remembered context: "Water Treatment", not a sentence about last week's
  open items.
- A field helper names its field: "Password needs at least 6 characters", not "At least 6
  characters".
- A status sets a concrete expectation, such as how long a wait lasts.
- An empty state says what to do or whom to contact, never internal state.
- Prefer the compact noun: "finish signup" over "finish signing up".

## First-run exception

The first memo a new user sees about what the product is gets two or three plain sentences under
a standard heading such as "Welcome to Stellis". A step that changes what the user is looking at,
such as a proposed setup, gets one short orienting sentence; a bare label alone reads as out of
the blue.
